Thirty Tabs

A survey of the tracker spreadsheet: what writes each tab, what reads it, which ones are permanent, and which third of it is derived and could be regenerated tomorrow. Nothing here has been changed.

This was assembled by reading every mention of every tab constant in the tracker's 13,373 lines and sorting them into who writes and who reads, then downloading the live workbook and checking the answer against it. It is a description, not a plan — the plan is at the bottom, and none of it happens without your say-so.

The code knows about thirty tabs. There are fifty-two. Twenty-two of them nothing in the tracker has ever heard of: five the portal made, two marked LEGACY V18, and fifteen called “Form Responses N” holding around 546 rows between them.

And one deleted row has been quietly breaking eight tabs. A single cell on the phase engine reads =IF(#REF!="","",#REF!), and because every column on that tab keys off it, that one cell takes out its whole row on seven more. Eighty-eight broken cells, one cause, no error message anywhere. That is the “behaves unpredictably” complaint, and it is at the bottom of this page.

What the survey turned up

Four things about the spreadsheet itself, separate from the code defects already fixed.

One deleted row is breaking eighty-eight cells across eight tabs

06 PHASE · STATUS ENGINE is the tab every view is built from, and its column A is the key — all twenty-three columns beside it open with IF($A{row}="","", …). One cell in that column reads =IF(#REF!="","",#REF!). A row was deleted from the trainee master at some point and Google rewrote that formula; nothing said so.

One cell takes out its whole row on the engine, and then on tabs 07, 08, 09, 10, 11, 13 and HOME. A second cell in the same column is crossed — it reads a different trainee's row than the one it sits on.

Now findable: engineHealthCheck() names both. previewEngineRepairV20_6() shows the repair; applyEngineRepairV20_6(token) does it, and touches nothing but that one column.

Two rows on the trainee master hold a clearance and no name

Every formula on the engine opens with IF($A{row}="","", …), so a row with an empty name column is silently blank on every view. Not missing — invisible, which looks identical from outside and is not the same thing. One of the two carries an email address and a status of “Cleared to Independent Practice”.

Putting a name back is a change to a personnel record. The health check reports it and does nothing about it, which is the correct division of labour.

Fifteen “Form Responses” tabs, around 546 rows

Google mints one of these every time a form is relinked, and they accumulate. Four are substantial — 138, 127, 117 and 115 rows. Nothing in the code reads them by name, which is correct and deliberate, so nothing tells you what is in them or whether it also reached the record.

They are the Google-owned copy of responses, not necessarily lost data — but they are a second copy of personnel information sitting unprotected under names nobody chose.

Tab 12's header row is a column short of its data

Column D is blank, so every label from D rightward names the column to its left. Anything reading that tab by header is reading a neighbour.

A previewed tool for this already exists — repairDecisionQueueHeaderV20_4() — and has not been run. It checks for an OWNER column to decide whether it has already fixed things, and the live sheet has none.

Tab 17 held permanent records and was not protected — fixed

Eight of the ten record tabs are locked so only the script may write them. 17 TRAINEE ARCHIVE is not, and nothing about an archive of released trainees wants hand-editing. 13 AUDIT · EXCEPTION LOG is also unprotected, but that one looks deliberate — columns J and K exist to be ticked by a person.

Five tabs were named by two constants each — fixed

15, 16, 17, 22 and 23 each have two names in the code pointing at the same sheet. On its own that is untidy. Combined with the four names in doubt it is a trap: change one constant to match a renamed tab and the other still points at the old name, and the half that still works hides the half that does not.

Withdrawn: the four tab names were fine all along

The sheet titles read “03 TRAINEE SELF-REFLECTION RAW”, “07 WEEKLY TRAINEE STATUS REPORT”, “10 TRAINING DIVISION DASHBOARD” and “13 AUDIT / EXCEPTION LOG”. Those are cell A1, not the tab names. The workbook manifest settles it: all four constants match their tabs exactly.

Withdrawn too: the help text is not stored on tab 10. That cell is ='00 README - CONTROL'!F5 — it already lives on tab 00, which is where I proposed moving it to. It is only shown in the middle of the dashboard, which is how it got copied, but there is nothing to move.

And what is right, which is most of it

  • All fourteen positional reads into tab 02 check out against the live header row.
  • The urgent-concern and self-reflection alerts map every field correctly.
  • Every response written into a tab is passed through the formula-injection guard.
  • No caller of the sign-off writer holds a lock — the single-level lock rule holds.
  • Every destructive tool is token-gated and shows you what it would do first.
  • Tab 00 is read by label, never by fixed row, which is why moving a setting has never broken it.

What I would do, in this order

Cheapest and least reversible first is the wrong order. This is least risky first, and every step is independently useful — you can stop after any of them.

  1. Reads only

    Run engineHealthCheck()

    Names the broken cell, the crossed one, and the master rows that hold data and no name. This is the one that explains the symptoms you have been living with.

  2. Reads only

    Run previewEngineRepairV20_6()

    Shows the exact before and after of every cell it would change, and writes nothing.

  3. One column, one tab

    Apply the engine repair

    Restores each cell of the key column to read its own row of the master, which is what every undamaged cell in it already does. Nothing else is touched — the columns beside it and the seven tabs downstream recalculate themselves.

  4. Personnel record

    Decide what the two nameless rows are

    One has a clearance and an address and no name. Only you know who that is, and putting a name on a personnel record is not something a tool should do on its own.

  5. Reads only

    Run tabNameCheck()

    Now mostly a listing of the twenty-two tabs the code has never heard of — the fifteen “Form Responses”, the two LEGACY V18, and the five the portal made.

  6. Reads only

    Run repairDecisionQueueHeaderV20_4() for its preview

    Tab 12's blank column D. It reports before it writes.

  7. Removes tabs

    Decide whether tabs 08, 09 and 11 still earn their place

    They are the v19 per-role views: FTO, trainee, Medical Director. The portal now does exactly that job for each of those three, per person, with access control the spreadsheet cannot enforce — tab 11 shows scores, and the portal's Medical Director screen deliberately does not. They are also three of the eight tabs the broken cell reaches.

    Two views of the same records that disagree is worse than one. But this is a judgement about how you work, not a defect, so it is last and it is yours.

What I will not do without you saying so

  • Rename any tab. A form writes to three of them, and Google mints a new response tab on relink.
  • Touch the fifteen “Form Responses” tabs. Until something has read what is in them and matched it against the ingestion ledger, deleting one could be deleting the only copy of a submission.
  • Put a name on a nameless row on the trainee master.
  • Delete any tab, derived or not.
  • Touch a record tab's contents by any route other than a previewed, token-gated tool.
  • Change what tab 06 computes. It is the one derived tab the rest of the system reads back, so it behaves like a record even though it is not one.
